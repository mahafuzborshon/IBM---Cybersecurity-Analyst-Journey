# Module 02: Analyzing PoS and Insider Breach Case Studies

In this module, I explored two major types of cybersecurity incidents: Point of Sale (PoS) breaches and insider breaches. Through real-world case studies, I learned how attackers exploit weaknesses in payment systems and how trusted insiders can unintentionally or intentionally cause serious security incidents.

I first learned about Point of Sale (PoS) breaches and how cybercriminals use malware and skimming techniques to steal customers' payment card information. I understood how attackers target PoS systems and why organizations must secure payment environments to protect sensitive financial data.

The Target and Home Depot case studies helped me understand how large-scale PoS breaches occur. I analyzed how attackers gained access to internal networks, deployed PoS malware, collected payment card data, and remained undetected for extended periods. I also learned how the Cyber Kill Chain can be used to analyze each stage of an attack.

The second part of the module focused on insider breaches. I learned that insider threats can originate from employees, contractors, or business partners who have legitimate access to organizational resources. These threats may be malicious, negligent, or accidental, making them difficult to detect.

I also studied the Vault 7 and Pegasus Airlines case studies, which demonstrated how insider actions and poor security controls can expose highly sensitive information. These examples reinforced the importance of monitoring privileged access, protecting confidential data, and implementing strong insider threat detection strategies.

Overall, this module strengthened my understanding of payment system attacks, insider threats, attack analysis, and the security controls organizations should implement to reduce the risk of both external and internal cyber breaches.
