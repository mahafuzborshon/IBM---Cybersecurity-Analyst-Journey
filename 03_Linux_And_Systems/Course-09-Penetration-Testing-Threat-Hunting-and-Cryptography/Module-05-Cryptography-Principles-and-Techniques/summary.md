# Module 05: Cryptography: Principles and Techniques

In this module, I learned how cryptography protects sensitive information by ensuring confidentiality, integrity, authentication, and data security. I explored the evolution of cryptography and understood why it remains one of the most important components of modern cybersecurity.

I learned the differences between encryption, decryption, and hashing. Encryption converts readable data into ciphertext to prevent unauthorized access, while decryption restores the original information for authorized users. Hashing, on the other hand, generates a fixed-length value that helps verify data integrity without revealing the original content.

Another important topic was symmetric and asymmetric encryption. I learned that symmetric encryption uses the same key for both encryption and decryption, making it fast and suitable for large amounts of data. In contrast, asymmetric encryption uses a pair of public and private keys, providing secure communication and digital authentication.

The hands-on labs gave me practical experience with AES and RSA encryption. I also explored how RSA supports secure key exchange and how AES is widely used for efficient data encryption in real-world systems.

This module also introduced me to common cryptographic attacks and cryptanalysis techniques. I learned how weak encryption, poor key management, and implementation flaws can compromise security. I also studied SSL/TLS, secure key management practices, and the importance of protecting cryptographic keys.

Finally, I explored quantum-safe cryptography and IBM Quantum Safe Cryptography Solutions. I learned that future quantum computers may break many existing encryption algorithms, making quantum-resistant cryptographic techniques increasingly important for long-term cybersecurity.

Overall, this module strengthened my understanding of modern cryptographic techniques, encryption algorithms, hashing, secure communication, and the future challenges of protecting information in a rapidly evolving threat landscape.
